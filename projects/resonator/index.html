Compact Toroidal RF Resonator

A modeling-to-measurement study of a directly tapped, open-loop toroidal resonator for compact trapped-ion RF delivery.

  • Room-temperature bench characterized
  • 4 K / UHV validation not completed
  • Feedback sensing in design

01

Problem

A Sandia-developed three-zone Peregrine trap provides the application context: RF delivery near 40 MHz and 250 V, with less volume than the existing helical resonator. The engineering question was whether a toroidal device could provide a compact, tunable path without hiding parasitic loss behind an ideal circuit model.

02

My role

Starting from a collaborator-provided prototype concept, my work focused on reinterpreting the coupling mechanism, developing the open-loop redesign, building the COMSOL/SPICE calibration workflow, extracting geometry-dependent parameters, and carrying the design through room-temperature characterization.

The project produced a 25-page design report and a 41-slide technical presentation.

03

Constraints

  • Compactness: reduce RF-drive footprint while moving toward the approximately 40 MHz operating regime.
  • Retunability: place the matching capacitor outside the future 4 K UHV chamber so adjustment does not require reopening it. This is design intent, not a completed cryogenic implementation.
  • Loading: the RF delivery system requires resonator-voltage feedback. Any capacitive-divider pickoff and downstream rectifier must respect the strong resonance shift caused by added capacitance.
  • Loss: connector resistance, PCB implementation, and full-wave structural effects can dominate behavior omitted by ideal matching theory.

04

Methods

The project moved through a sequence of engineering judgments: reject an ineffective physical interpretation, reshape the topology, extract a compact model from full-wave simulations, then test whether the resulting design trends survive on the bench.

05

Validation

Two VNA prototypes

Two different parameter sets in the same directly tapped, open-loop topology measured Q = 38 at 22.66 MHz and Q = 58 at 37.15 MHz.

The higher-frequency, smaller-scale build also produced the higher measured Q, consistent with the central geometry trend of the calibrated model.

Separate connectorized assembly

A different test assembly used the Q = 58 parameter set but replaced direct PCB soldering with removable screw connectors. Added connection resistance reduced and stabilized Q at approximately 45. On that hardware, a low-capacitance probe directly measured approximately 13× and 14× voltage gain at probe-loaded resonances of 19.55 MHz and 30.3 MHz.

The Q = 38 / Q = 58 values and the 13× / 14× gains therefore do not describe one physical assembly.

06

Results

  • Topology decision: the directly tapped, open-loop interpretation replaced the ineffective floating-coupling picture.
  • Modeling decision: a calibrated full-wave-to-lumped workflow replaced reliance on ideal matching theory alone.
  • Measured trend: among the two VNA prototypes, the higher-frequency design also produced the higher Q.
  • Integration lesson: removable connectors improved serviceability but introduced enough resistance to reduce Q.
  • Sensing constraint: probe and pickoff capacitance cannot be treated as invisible; it measurably moves resonance.

07

Limitations

The work establishes room-temperature behavior, not completed operation at 4 K or in UHV. The external matching-capacitor arrangement remains a design intent. The voltage-feedback subsystem—capacitive-divider pickoff plus downstream rectifier—is still being designed and has not been bench-validated.

Further work should add a calibration and uncertainty budget for connectorized gain measurements, repeated-device and tolerance studies, cryogenic/thermal tests, and reproducible measurement assets.
