Reduced Internal-State Dynamics for an Indium MOT

A seven-level QuTiP/Lindblad study of optical pumping, spontaneous decay, and repumping in the internal level structure of 115In.

  • Seven-level Lindblad model
  • NIST + literature transition inputs
  • Numerical only · no experimental validation

01

Problem

Indium does not offer a convenient closed cooling transition from its ground state. Published work instead used a long-lived 5P3/2 metastable manifold, a 325.7 nm cooling transition, and multiple repumping paths to realize an experimental magneto-optical trap.

This course project asked a narrower modeling question: how much of the internal-state population flow could be understood with a computationally manageable open-quantum-system model?

Scope boundary

The published experiment supplied the physical context. This project did not build, operate, or measure a MOT, laser system, magnetic-field coil, vacuum chamber, or cold-atom apparatus.

02

My role

The final report was completed with Son Pham. The main report body was team work.

I developed the QuTiP simulation, wrote the technical appendix, produced all simulation figures, calculated the Landé g-factors and hyperfine branching ratios, and found and organized the references and NIST Atomic Spectra Database data used by the model.

03

A reduced seven-level model

Within the course scope, I deliberately coarse-grained 95 magnetic sublevels into seven hyperfine-level states to isolate population-flow mechanisms and keep the model interpretable. This abstraction was a modeling choice, not a claim that the omitted spatial, Zeeman, or polarization physics was negligible.

Internal states included in the QuTiP model
Manifold Included F levels Modeled role
5P1/2 F = 4, 5 Ground-state population and repumping inputs
5P3/2 F = 4, 5, 6 Metastable levels; F = 6 is the lower cooling state
6S1/2 F = 5 Intermediate repumping state
5D5/2 F = 7 Upper cooling state

Level energies came from NIST ASD data and published hyperfine splittings. Fine-structure transition rates were combined with Wigner-6j strengths calculated in Python to construct hyperfine branching ratios. The time evolution was solved with QuTiP's master-equation solver.

dρ dt = −i [ H0 + Hint (t) ,ρ ] + ∑k ( Ck ρ Ck† − 12 { Ck† Ck ,ρ } ) \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -i[H_0 + H_{\mathrm{int}}(t),\rho] + \sum_k\left(C_k\rho C_k^\dagger - \frac{1}{2}\{C_k^\dagger C_k,\rho\}\right)
  • Coherent drive: time-dependent Hamiltonian terms represented the cooling and repumping lasers.
  • Population loss and transfer: collapse operators represented spontaneous decay between included levels.
  • Dark-state diagnostic: additional dephasing operators tested whether coherent population trapping was an artifact of perfectly phase-coherent simulated drives.

04

Parameter studies

The final notebook became a working parameter-study file: after the report was completed, individual detunings, dephasing rates, leakage values, initial states, and laser combinations were repeatedly changed to inspect different regimes. The saved plots preserve the report-aligned comparisons more clearly than the notebook's last parameter state.

05

What the simulation showed

A diagnostic sequence, not experimental validation

Modeled dephasing removed the strong dark-state behavior produced by perfectly coherent drives and allowed the state-preparation simulation to move population toward 5P3/2, F = 6.

At the literature-scale leakage probability, no recognizable loss emerged over the simulated microsecond window. Artificially amplifying that channel by 1000× exposed the relative role of the repumping lasers.

  • Modeling lesson: coherent population trapping can dominate a simplified simulation when independent experimental laser phases are represented as perfectly coherent.
  • Timescale lesson: a physically small leakage channel may be invisible in a short numerical run even when it matters over much longer operation.
  • Diagnostic lesson: deliberately exaggerating one mechanism can reveal causal differences between control configurations, provided the result is not presented as a physical forecast.

07

Limitations

The model omits magnetic sublevels, spatial motion, Zeeman shifts, polarization selection, laser-beam geometry, atomic collisions, and the position-dependent restoring force that makes a MOT a trap. It therefore cannot predict temperature, cloud size, capture efficiency, atom number, or an experimental trap lifetime.

The figures are numerical outputs, not measured data and not experimental validation. The final notebook was later reused for parameter exploration, so its saved last state is not treated as one canonical benchmark configuration. This page reports the course-report conclusions together with the corresponding saved plots.
